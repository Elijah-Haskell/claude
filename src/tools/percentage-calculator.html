Answer: —

The formulas

  • X% of Y = X ÷ 100 × Y. Example: 15% of 80 = 0.15 × 80 = 12.
  • X is what percent of Y = X ÷ Y × 100. Example: 42 out of 50 = 84%.
  • Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ |old| × 100. Example: 40 → 50 is a 25% increase; 50 → 40 is a 20% decrease.

Common uses for students

  • Turn a test score into a percentage: points earned is what percent of points possible.
  • Work out a discount or tip: X% of the price.
  • Compare data in a lab report or assignment: percentage change between two measurements.

FAQ

Why isn't a 25% increase undone by a 25% decrease?
Because the second percentage is taken from a different starting number. 40 → 50 is +25%, but 50 → 40 is only −20% of 50.
What's the difference between percent and percentage points?
Going from 10% to 12% is a 2 percentage-point rise, but a 20% increase in the rate itself.
